## Research Overview

My clinical motion-analysis work investigates how ordinary gait videos can provide quantitative and interpretable evidence of movement-related disorders. An important application is the **assessment of adult spinal deformity (ASD)**, where a person's gait can reveal functional characteristics that are not fully captured by static imaging.

I study **spatiotemporal learning**, **phase-aware motion representations**, and **clinical knowledge-guided attention**. These methods connect visual cues with domain knowledge—for example, emphasizing joints or body regions identified as diagnostically meaningful by clinicians—while evaluating performance on patient gait recordings.

### Selected Contributions

- **Two-stage video-based CNNs (2023):** Spatiotemporal gait video classification for adult spinal deformity. [*Frontiers in Neuroscience*](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2023.1278584)
- **PhaseMix (2024):** Periodic motion fusion to improve gait-video classification. [*IEEE Access*](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10714330)
- **FilterNet (2026):** Selection of phase-relevant frames for periodic motion analysis. [*ICEIC 2026*](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICEIC69189.2026.11386438)
- **Clinical knowledge-guided attention (2026):** An anatomy-informed attention framework evaluated on monocular gait videos. [*SPIE Medical Imaging*](https://doi.org/10.1117/12.3083822) · [*Journal of Medical Imaging*](https://doi.org/10.1117/1.JMI.13.5.054002)

These methods are intended as **research tools for quantitative assessment and potential screening support**, not replacements for clinical diagnosis.
